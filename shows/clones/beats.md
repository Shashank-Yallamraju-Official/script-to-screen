# Six of One: story beats

A working title. A thriller about self-identity, told with six women who share one genome and one house.

## What it says

You are not what you were made from, and you are not what you were made to be like. A self is what you do
with what you were given, and nobody can choose it for you. Nobody in it is good or bad, the people who
built the house included.

## The device

The house was built to watch them. One wall of every room is one-way glass, with a corridor behind it. The
viewer is always behind that glass. A character looking out is looking in a mirror. Anything that crosses
the foreground is someone in the corridor with us.

## The six

Each carries one of the Big Five traits turned all the way up, and each starts at one of Marcia's identity
statuses. What each needs is what another has too much of.

| | Trait turned up | Starts as | Tells herself | Takes, by the end |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nell, "the original" | None: middling in all five | Diffused: never explored, never chose | "I'm nobody in particular." | A decision, from Bex |
| Bex | Extraversion | Achieved, and built on being one of a kind | "There's only one of me." | Stillness, from Tess |
| Cora | Agreeableness | Foreclosed, by duty | "I'm whatever they need." | A refusal, from Bex |
| Greta | Conscientiousness | Foreclosed, by rules | "I do what's right." | A doubt, from Pia |
| Pia | Openness | Moratorium: always searching | "I'll know it when I find it." | A commitment, from Greta |
| Tess | Neuroticism | Moratorium, forced on her by fear | "If I watch, I'm safe." | The front door, from Cora |

Outside the six: Viv, who raised Nell in the house and died a month before the story starts; and Abel, the
study's last observer, who has watched all six for thirty years and has been told to close it.

## The beats

Six parts. Each has one arrival, one room, one mirror and one turn of the screw.

### Part 1. The hall

1. Nell alone in the house, a month after Viv's death. Nothing sorted, the study still locked. She drifts
   from room to room and stops at each mirror.
2. A knock. A woman with her face: Bex, holding a photograph of this house with her own face in the window.
3. Bex is furious, not frightened. Someone is using her face. Nell has no answer and no anger, and Bex takes
   that for guilt.
4. While they argue, something dark crosses the foreground. They do not see it. We are not alone behind the
   glass.

The mirror: Bex is Nell's ideal self, someone who decided. Nell is Bex's feared one, someone ordinary.
The psychology: the need to be distinct, attacked head on.

### Part 2. The kitchen

5. Morning. Cora arrives with food and an apology for arriving. Then Greta, with a folder, a list and a
   timetable.
6. Four at the table. They find they hold a cup the same way. They find they do not laugh at the same
   things. Bex and Greta both expect to be in charge.
7. Greta's folder holds ten years of "follow-up health forms" she has filled in and posted. Cora had a free
   clinic. Bex had a sponsor. Each of them has been reporting on herself.
8. Bex goes to leave. The front door does not open. It locks from the other side.

The mirror: Cora is the self Nell was told to be. Greta is the life lived by instruction.
The psychology: what twins raised apart share and do not; and how fast four people sort into an order.

### Part 3. The front room

9. Pia comes in over the garden wall, having never considered the door. Within a minute she has noticed the
   mirrors are wrong: too thick, and no dust shadow behind them.
10. The photograph wall. Nell's whole life is on it. There is no place on it for the others, and each of
    them stands in front of it a little too long.
11. Bex names it: Nell is the original. She had the house and the mother. The rest were given away. Nell
    does not deny it, because she has always assumed it.
12. Pia gets a panel open. Behind the glass: a corridor, a camp bed, and a cup of tea that is still warm.

The mirror: Pia is who Greta is afraid of becoming, and who Cora wishes she were.
The psychology: a life as a story on a wall, and the envy of people who have no wall.

### Part 4. Her old bedroom

13. They catch Tess in the corridor. She was sent her photograph a year ago, and has been watching the
    house for weeks without daring to knock.
14. Tess knows the most and is believed the least. She knows the study's name, Abel's name, and the date it
    closes: dawn, the day after tomorrow.
15. Having watched them all, she tells each one the thing about herself that she cannot see. Nobody thanks
    her.
16. The doorframe. Height marks, in pencil. Nell has always known them as hers. Tess has counted them: some
    are dated before Nell was born. They are Viv's. This was her childhood home too.
17. So there is no original among them. Viv was the original. Nell was raised by her own older self.
18. A knock at the front door, from outside. Abel: final assessment. Nobody is to leave until it is done.

The mirror: Tess is the one all of them are afraid of being.
The psychology: what others can see in us that we cannot; and five lives as the roads one woman did not
take.

### Part 5. The study

19. They break into the study. Six folders. Each of them was an arm of the study, placed in a life meant to
    turn one thing up. Viv's own folder is there too: a life chosen for her, an illness, and a question she
    ran out of time to answer herself.
20. Abel must see "the home subject" alone. Nell cannot face it. Bex goes in as Nell.
21. Bex holds Nell's stillness for almost a minute. Then her own way of standing leaks out. Abel has watched
    her since she was four. He knows at once, and says nothing.
22. Someone unlocked the back door for him. Blame runs to Tess, the outsider. It was Greta, who was told it
    was procedure and believed that procedure would keep them safe.
23. Abel, to all of them: at dawn the files are destroyed, the house is sold, and each goes back to her own
    life, with no contact, so as not to spoil thirty years of results. That is the kind version.

The mirror: Bex has to be Nell, and finds it is harder than being herself.
The psychology: obedience to authority; blame finding the outsider; and a mask that slips under pressure.

### Part 6. The corridor

24. Night. All six behind the glass, looking into their own empty rooms. For the first time they see the
    house as they were seen.
25. Each takes the one thing she lacks from the one who has too much of it. Greta breaks a rule and hands
    out the files. Cora says no to someone. Pia says she is staying. Bex listens. Tess walks to the front
    door.
26. Nell, who has never chosen anything, chooses. The house is hers, and so is what it is for.
27. Abel gives them the keys and writes six words in the last report: all subjects lost to follow-up. It
    costs him the only work of his life.
28. Viv's letter, found last: she wanted to know which of them she was. None of them. They were never her.
29. Nell hangs a curtain across the glass. The picture goes dark. They are, at last, not being watched, and
    the story ends because we cannot see it.

The mirror: all six, and the glass itself.
The psychology: a self as something chosen and told, and who you are when nobody is looking.
